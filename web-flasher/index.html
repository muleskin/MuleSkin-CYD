What changed in each release

    The 2.8" CYD ships with one of two different display drivers depending on manufacturing batch, and there's no way to tell which from the outside. If you flash one and get a solid white screen, that's not broken — just flash the other 2.8" option above instead.

    • Cheap Yellow Displays have a code like ESP32-2432S028R on the back. The last letter is the touch: R resistive (press), C capacitive (tap).
    • Freenove boards say FNK0103 or FNK0104.
    • Not on the list? Tell us what it is on GitHub.
    Your browser can't do this. Flashing needs Web Serial — Firefox, Chrome, Edge or another Chromium browser, on a computer. Phones and tablets can't do it at all, whatever browser they're running. This page needs to be loaded over HTTPS to flash a device.
    1. Plug your MuleSkin-CYD into this computer over USB.
    2. Click Connect & Install above.
    3. Pick the right serial port from the browser's popup.
    4. Click Install and leave it be — don't unplug until it says done.

    Works in Firefox / Chrome / Edge / Brave on desktop. No app to install — just the browser.

    Moving to v3.2.0 or later from an older version: install over USB here without ticking “Erase device” and the board moves its settings into its new, larger store by itself on the first boot.

    Back up settings before a reinstall that erases the board, and Restore settings after it: saved WiFi networks, colour order, time zone, push alerts and the rest come back. The backup stays in this browser (and can be saved as a file); it includes WiFi passwords and the push topic and token, so keep the file to yourself. A locked board has to be unlocked on its screen first. Needs firmware v3.1.3 or later.

    Download log saves every detection the board has kept (it survives restarts) as a spreadsheet file. Crash report lists the last boots and why each one happened -- a crash, a brownout, a restart -- with the details a bug report needs.

    Push only these types (and WITH YOU, always). None ticked: PUSH WHAT as set on the board.

    Push options: which detection types PUSH ALERTS sends (the board's PUSH WHAT becomes CHOSEN), and quiet hours for PUSH AT NIGHT other than the screen's NIGHT DIM. Needs firmware v3.2.4 or later.

    Ignored devices lists the devices the board never raises an alert for -- your own AirTag, your own doorbell -- and lets you remove one or add one by its address. They are still scanned and logged. The board stays connected until DONE. Needs firmware v3.2.2 or later.

    Away days lists the trackers, away from their owner, that the board has heard on days it was away from home -- three days makes the NEAR YOU ON N DAYS alert -- and the home network it goes by, with how many of its routers it has learned. Remove a tag you know (or IGNORE it for good). Needs firmware v3.4.0 or later.

    Self-test checks the board's parts -- memory, touch calibration, both radios, the black box, SD card, settings, clock, WiFi, update keys -- and lists anything that needs a look. Useful right after flashing a new board.

    Live view shows what the board is detecting right now, on this computer, while it stays plugged in -- each device's type, name, signal and how long ago it was heard. Press it again to stop.

    Push alerts send every alert to your phone as a real notification, lock screen and all, through the free ntfy app (iPhone or Android). The board joins one of its saved WiFi networks for a couple of seconds to send each one -- home, work, or your phone's hotspot -- so save those in WIFI NETWORKS first. Then install ntfy on the phone, subscribe to the topic below, and send it to the board over the cable. It sends a test message straight away.

    After flashing, Set Time & Zone tells the board this computer's clock and time zone over the same cable. The zone is kept for good; the clock lasts until the board is unplugged, and a saved WiFi network puts it back at every boot. A T-Watch has its clock set for it after installing, and keeps the time from then on, even through a flat battery.

    MuleSkin-CYD is an open-source surveillance detector for the ESP32 "Cheap Yellow Display" board.

    It is a fork of SquachWatch-CYD, created by skizzophrenic. The detection engine and its signatures, the board ports, the mesh, the simulator and this web flasher itself all began as skizzophrenic's work; MuleSkin rebrands it and reworks the look and the main screen. Full credit to skizzophrenic — like the original, it is free software under the GPL-3.0.

    Board not showing up as a port? Most CYDs use a CH340 USB chip — grab the driver: Windows · macOS · Linux — most Mac/Linux/ChromeOS machines already have this built in, it's mainly a Windows thing.

    Stay muleskin out there. · Built on SquachWatch-CYD by skizzophrenic.
