On the latest build and stuck in a boot loop? Flash the one before it.

The 2.8" CYD ships with one of two different display drivers depending on manufacturing batch, and there's no way to tell which from the outside. If you flash one and get a solid white screen, that's not broken — just flash the other 2.8" option above instead.

  • Cheap Yellow Displays have a code like ESP32-2432S028R on the back. The last letter is the touch: R resistive (press), C capacitive (tap).
  • Freenove boards say FNK0103 or FNK0104.
  • Not on the list? Tell us what it is on GitHub.
Your browser can't do this. Flashing needs Web Serial — Firefox, Chrome, Edge or another Chromium browser, on a computer. Phones and tablets can't do it at all, whatever browser they're running. This page needs to be loaded over HTTPS to flash a device.
  1. Plug your MuleSkin-CYD into this computer over USB.
  2. Click Connect & Install above.
  3. Pick the right serial port from the browser's popup.
  4. Click Install and leave it be — don't unplug until it says done.

Works in Firefox / Chrome / Edge / Brave on desktop. No app to install — just the browser.

After flashing, Set Time & Zone tells the board this computer's clock and time zone over the same cable. The zone is kept for good; the clock lasts until the board is unplugged, and a saved WiFi network puts it back at every boot. A T-Watch has its clock set for it after installing, and keeps the time from then on, even through a flat battery.

MuleSkin-CYD is an open-source surveillance detector for the ESP32 "Cheap Yellow Display" board.

It is a fork of SquachWatch-CYD, created by skizzophrenic. The detection engine and its signatures, the board ports, the mesh, the simulator and this web flasher itself all began as skizzophrenic's work; MuleSkin rebrands it and reworks the look and the main screen. Full credit to skizzophrenic — like the original, it is free software under the GPL-3.0.

Board not showing up as a port? Most CYDs use a CH340 USB chip — grab the driver: Windows · macOS · Linux — most Mac/Linux/ChromeOS machines already have this built in, it's mainly a Windows thing.
